A close has four stages. Nothing is signed until you have seen the whole plan.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Analyze">
    You enter the account to close, a destination, and the network. LumenWipe reads the live
    on-chain state: balances, trustlines, offers, data entries, signers, sponsorships, claimable
    balances, and DeFi positions. For each asset you choose what happens to it: convert to XLM,
    return to the issuer, or transfer to an account you name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review">
    The review page shows the complete ordered plan, the estimated fee, and the estimated XLM that
    reaches the destination. No transaction exists yet, and nothing is saved for resuming until you
    confirm. See [Interruptions are safe](#interruptions-are-safe).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign">
    Confirming the review builds the first transaction. Before your signer ever sees it, the browser
    decodes it and checks that it does exactly what you asked, against your own inputs and not the
    server's response. A mismatch aborts before signing. See [signing
    options](/guides/signing-options).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Execute">
    The signed transaction is submitted and confirmed. If more work remains, the next round starts
    from fresh on-chain state, until the account is merged.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The plan

For a given account state the plan is always the same. It runs in this order, which satisfies the ledger's constraints:

1. Exit DeFi positions, each as its own transaction
2. Normalize signers, when extra signers exist. When no single key of the account can sign the close, this comes first instead, as a transaction of its own, so the master key alone signs every step after it
3. Revoke the sponsorships this account provides, when the owners can absorb the reserve
4. Remove data entries
5. Cancel offers
6. Claim claimable balances
7. Handle each asset
8. Remove trustlines
9. Merge the account

Liquidity pool shares are not unwound by LumenWipe. An account that holds them is reported as a blocker, and the shares have to be withdrawn with another tool first.

## One transaction, or several

Most accounts close in a single transaction, signed once. More are needed in a few cases:

- **An exchange destination** adds one transaction, the [mediator](/reference/glossary#mediator) transfer. See [exchange closes](/guides/exchanges-and-the-mediator).
- **An account no single key can sign** removes its other signers first, in a transaction of its own, so the rest of the close needs only the master key.
- **Claimable balances** are claimed first and the account is closed second, so the proceeds are not lost.
- **A Soroban step**, such as a DeFi exit or a Soroswap aggregator swap, is its own transaction, because it cannot be mixed with other operations.
- **More than 100 operations** are split into the fewest transactions the protocol limit allows.

## Interruptions are safe

A close you can resume exists only after you confirm on the review page. Before that, your choices, destination and memo are kept only in this tab, so a reload of the analyze or review page shows them again for you to check, and the plan is built again from your account. A confirmation that gives value up, such as leaving a DeFi position behind, is always asked again. Closing the tab leaves nothing to resume, and nothing has happened.

After you confirm, your browser keeps a small record of the close: the account, the destination, the memo, how many steps confirmed with their transaction hashes, and the hash of any transaction still waiting for the network. It never keeps your keys, a signed transaction or the plan.

The server keeps no progress between rounds. Each round re-reads the account and works out what remains. If your browser closes or a submission fails after you confirmed, resume from the start page: the account is read again and a fresh plan is built for what is left, which you review and sign again. Work that already confirmed is not repeated, because it is no longer on the account. A transaction that may still be waiting is checked by its hash first.
